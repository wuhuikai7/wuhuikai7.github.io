Award-Winning Bourbon and Local Craft: The Ambition, Struggles, and Community Spirit of Missouri’s Distilling Industry

Richard, from Rieger distillery company, is introducing different kinds of local bourbon, taken by Huikai Wu

COLUMBIA, Mo. — Since the full implementation of the Missouri Bourbon Act, the state's craft spirits industry has been undergoing a structural reshaping driven by both federal and state-level regulations. According to the latest economic data from the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau (TTB), the American Craft Spirits Association (ACSA), and the University of Missouri Extension (MU Extension), this industry model — rooted in the protection of geographic origin — has transformed Missouri from a simple producer of agricultural raw materials into a premier export hub for high value-added spirits.

In the whiskey distilleries of Kansas City, bourbon is undergoing multi-layered distillation and meticulous processing. When consumers across the United States savor these richly layered whiskeys, they may be reminded of the origin of these spirits: Missouri. The rolling Ozark Mountains of the Midwest, vacationers enjoying drinks by the Lake of the Ozarks, the massive brewing machinery in Hermann, the bustling beer festivals in St. Louis, and the signature bourbon of Kansas City together form the most vivid annotation of the Missouri distilling industry.

The Production and Export of Missouri Bourbon

"In 2018, a few of us founded the Missouri Craft Distillers Guild together," said David Weglarz, president of the guild and founder of StilL 630 distillery in St. Louis. "In 2019, we helped push a bill, House Bill 266, to become law, officially establishing Missouri bourbon as an actual whiskey category in a legal sense."

To be classified as Missouri bourbon, a spirit must first comply with all standard federal bourbon regulations: it must be made primarily from corn, aged in brand-new charred oak barrels, and manufactured in the United States.

However, Missouri has established an even higher threshold. "To be labeled as Missouri bourbon, all the corn used must be grown locally in Missouri. The oak barrels themselves must also be manufactured within the state," Weglarz emphasized. "Most importantly, the entire production process — from mashing, fermenting, and distilling, to aging, bottling, and labeling — must be completed within the exact same distillery in Missouri. Our state has extremely high-quality agricultural products and world-renowned oak barrels from the Ozark region. Coupled with excellent water quality, favorable weather, and highly talented distillers, these elements collectively create this outstanding whiskey."

This rigorous pursuit of origin has created a distinct flavor profile. Weglarz explained that the beauty of Missouri bourbon lies in its subtle nuances. "While they are all primarily made from corn, different distilleries will add a little rye and barley, or wheat, into the recipe. It’s just like how rye bread and wheat bread taste completely different. Even if the exact same grain recipe is used, because the equipment and distillers used by each distillery are different, the flavors will vary."

Medal wall from Saint Louis Still630 by David Weglarz

In terms of mouthfeel, consumers can expect to taste prominent notes of vanilla, caramel, and oak spice extracted from the Missouri oak barrels, layered with a mellow, rich sweet corn flavor. Weglarz noted: "The rye adds a bit of spice and bold flavor, while the barley provides a beautiful grain base that acts like scaffolding to support all the other flavors. Whether you are drinking it neat, or using it to make classic cocktails like a Manhattan or an Old Fashioned, it is a fantastic base spirit."

Weglarz also detailed the technical "distill on the grain" process used at StilL 630 and similar distilleries. "We first cook the grains to mash them, converting the starches into sugars. Most distilleries and breweries will separate the grain from the liquid. But we pump the entire contents, with all the grains included, directly into the fermentation tank, allowing the yeast to fully interact with it and absorb the sugars left on the grains. After fermentation is finished, we pump all the contents into the pot still and distill on the grain directly. This allows us to get a richer, more flavorful spirit, which is one of our core characteristics."

The Impressive Market Performance of the Missouri Bourbon Act

This regulation has directly boosted the state’s upstream agricultural and forestry sectors. According to data jointly released by the MU Extension and the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States (DISCUS), the production of Missouri craft bourbon increases the economic value of the state's corn from the farm to the consumer's hands by an average of eight times.

In terms of total economic output, the distilled spirits industry contributes over $567 million in annual total sales to Missouri's economy, with the total gross domestic product (GDP) value-added exceeding $357 million. The industry possesses a powerful employment multiplier effect: data shows that for every single direct job created by the Missouri distilling industry, it supports 1.9 related jobs in other industries across the state, such as forestry, transportation, and tourism.

Missouri Bourbon contributor, taken by David Weglarz

From a federal macroeconomic perspective, Missouri's market performance highly aligns with the national trend of craft spirits exports. According to the "2025 Craft Spirits Data Project" recently released by the ACSA, total annual sales in the national craft spirits market reached $7.58 billion.

In the cross-state transaction data of that report, 51.5% of all national craft spirits sales occurred outside the state of production. This export ratio, exceeding half of all sales, verifies the effectiveness of Missouri's "label premium" strategy. Missouri currently boasts more than 100 active craft distilleries, ranking 16th nationally.

The Truth About Profit Models: Born from Scratch, Sustained by Passion

Despite the glowing macro-level data, running a distillery is far from an easy business for individual owners.

"This industry is incredibly capital-intensive. You need to buy equipment and invest in oak barrels," Weglarz admitted. "If you are going to age spirits, you have to pay all the costs upfront and pray that in a few years you can recoup the costs and make just a little bit of profit. No one I know in this business is really getting rich. We are just maintaining survival, ensuring our employees get paid, and taking a little salary for ourselves."

In the city of Columbia, the Trailer Stop craft beer shop, which opened on April 1, 2025, faces similar financial pressures. The owner Robert Zitsch stated bluntly: "We haven't even made enough money to break even. You have to build the structure yourself, and you have to finance it with bank loans. Commercial real estate is very expensive. From day one of opening, the costs of the land and the building have already weighed on us, meaning you are already in debt right from the start."

Robert Zitsch, owner of trail stop, is busy keeping an eye on everything, taken by Huikai Wu

Operational costs are equally tedious and heavy: furniture, equipment, ingredients, and staff wages. "It takes a long time just to cover all the monthly expenses," the owner added. To optimize margins, Trailer Stop chooses not to sell canned beer, offering only draft beer directly from the keg. "While larger volumes can lower unit prices, canning itself is not cheap. Selling draft beer directly at the source may yield better margins than external distribution."

Joint Community Spirit and Ozark- Lake Lifestyle

What keeps these distillers going is a unique community spirit.

"The best part is that the distilleries in Missouri have formed a true community. We do not view each other as stealing sales," Weglarz said. "We pushed for the legislation together, and we facilitated the 'Missouri Spirits Expedition' project, which has driven tourism. Sometimes we buy grain together and share resources. It is an amazing spirit."

Rieger distillery company’s unique way of adding flavor to the whiskey, taken by Huikai Wu

This sense of pride is also evident among frontline staff. At the J. Rieger & Co. distillery in Kansas City, bartender Richard explained: "Our gin is incredibly smooth and balanced, unlike gin from other places. Some of our whiskeys, after multiple distillations, are placed into barrels that previously stored other spirits to referment, unlocking more flavors." He added enthusiastically: "Last New Year, we had 700 people visit at one time. After all, this is a place for fun!"

At the Lake of the Ozarks, the lifestyle manifests in a different form. Jonason Snelling, a military veteran and owner of Rip Tavern, focuses heavily on community care. "If people drink too much, we have a $25 towing service contact within 20 kilometers," Snelling said. "Seeing people enjoy a beautiful night in the Lake of the Ozarks area gives me a great sense of fulfillment."

Haha tonka park, Ozark lake area, a hot destination for summer, taken by Huikai Wu

Under the baking afternoon sun, the air at Dog Days Bar & Grill is filled with the scent of salted glass rims and sunscreen. Here, the Ozark lifestyle is served by the gallon. While Missouri's craft distillers delve into caramel-charred oak barrels and complex grain recipes in the cellars of Kansas City or St. Louis, Dog Days acts as the high-traffic frontline of the state's spirits economy.

On a multi-level open-air deck, the rhythmic hum of industrial blenders forms the background music for the weekend rush hour. Tourists, still dripping with lake water, line up at the bar waiting for the establishment's signature frozen cocktails and local craft beer. The meticulous craftsmanship of native Missouri IPAs merges here with the staggering consumption speeds of a holiday weekend.

For Missourians, time spent at a distillery is not just about the alcohol; it means leisure, happiness, and a deep sense of pride in what their home soil produces.

Stock Price Volatility Factors

1. December 2022, Musk's acquisition of X triggered massive sell-offs to raise cash, sending Tesla's stock price plummeting to $123

Musk's acquisition of Twitter (now X) not only diverted his attention but also forced him to repeatedly sell large quantities of Tesla stock to raise cash, sparking investor panic.

The Federal Reserve's aggressive interest rate hikes led to a broad valuation correction in tech stocks, with Tesla's then-high P/E ratio bearing the brunt of the impact.

Rising competition from rivals like BYD forced Tesla into a “price war,” causing profit margins to decline.

2. July 2024 Earnings Shock: Autonomous Driving Launch Delayed, Triggering Sell-off to $176

Despite Q2 revenue reaching $25.5 billion (year-over-year growth), weekly revenue showed a significant year-over-year decline. This was primarily due to sustained “price wars” and high costs pressuring automotive business margins.

During the earnings call, Musk confirmed the delay of the Robotaxi launch from August to October, significantly dampening short-term market hype around AI technology.

Investors were disappointed by sluggish growth in the company's core EV sales. While the energy storage business grew rapidly, it failed to offset the negative impact of declining automotive gross margins.

3. December 2024: Trump Takes Office, Eases Regulations, Boosting EV Leader Tesla to $403

Following Donald Trump's victory in the 2024 U.S. presidential election, the market widely anticipated policies including deregulation and the elimination of electric vehicle tax credits. Investors viewed this as favorable for industry leader Tesla, as while it restricted electric vehicles overall, it bolstered Tesla's competitive edge through high-profile support.

In mid-December, Tesla adjusted Model S pricing in certain regions (e.g., raising the all-wheel-drive version by $5,000), signaling robust demand exceeding supply expectations and triggering consecutive days of surging stock prices.

By mid-December, Tesla's stock price breached key technical levels (such as $420), driven by option traders' positions. This triggered a gamma squeeze, forcing short sellers to cover their positions by buying shares, further accelerating the stock's surge.

Musk's launch of xAI in late December brought major model announcements, boosting valuation expectations for Tesla as an AI and robotics company. Tesla's stock is no longer solely driven by its electric vehicle business; the market now values it as a pioneer in AI and humanoid robotics. Optimistic AI projections fueled overall valuation growth by late 2024.

4. Tesla's stock price reached an all-time high of approximately $490 in December 2025

Tesla no longer relied solely on vehicle sales, as its FSD (Full Self-Driving) subscription base surpassed 1.1 million users. This high-margin software revenue stream transformed investors' valuation models.

In early 2026, Tesla launched fully driverless Robotaxi operations in Austin, prompting the market to reclassify it as a “transportation platform company.”

2025 financial reports revealed that energy storage (Megapack) contributed nearly 25% of the company's total profits, providing robust support for its stock price.

5. 2026 Status: Trading Around $420

Bullish View: Focus on production line progress for the Optimus humanoid robot. Once robots enter factories for labor, Tesla's valuation potential will shift from “trillion” to “ten trillion.”

Bearish View: Monitor automotive gross margins. With diminishing FSD tax incentives and global EV penetration hitting a bottleneck, core vehicle sales margins (around 18%) remain below the peak 30%.

Summary: Tesla's Indestructible “Core Secret”

Tesla consistently rebounds after major declines because it repeatedly unveils the next “tech pie” when its core business (vehicle sales) hits growth bottlenecks—then delivers on it with hardcore execution (from Model 3 → Energy Storage → FSD → Optimus).

Economies of Scale

The core logic of economies of scale is: higher production volume reduces fixed costs per unit.

R&D Amortization: Developing a vehicle model costs $1 billion. Selling 10,000 units allocates $100,000 in R&D costs per vehicle. Selling 1.8 million units annually like Tesla reduces this to just a few hundred dollars per vehicle.

Tesla's massive procurement volumes (especially for battery materials) enable it to secure components at extremely low prices. In contrast, Rivian or Lucid, constrained by smaller scale, face higher procurement costs and slower production, resulting in losses per vehicle sold.

Tesla's Giga Press technology integrates 70 components into one. This requires massive upfront equipment investment, feasible only for companies with massive order volumes like Tesla. Once operational, its production speed and cost advantages leave competitors hopeless.

Boone County shifts jail tax vote to November as out-of-county housing costs project to hit $5 million amid booking surge

COLUMBIA, Mo. — The Boone County Commission has rescheduled a pivotal three-eighths-cent law enforcement sales tax election from August to the Nov. 3 general election ballot. The delay gives officials more time to launch a massive public education campaign regarding severe jail overcrowding, escalating judicial gridlock, and a dramatic spike in inmate outsourcing costs.

Boone County Sheriff Dwayne Carey requested the timeline shift following the Memorial Day weekend. Carey cited the logistical impossibility of presenting the county’s data to 40 to 50 local civic groups, rotary clubs, and municipal councils within a short summer window.

"We want people to understand the issue," Carey said, noting that the ballot initiative relies entirely on fact-based, verifiable data. "The more you can get factual information out, the better off you are, because there's so much misinformation that keyboard warriors on social media want to put out there."

The postponement also separates the county’s jail tax from the city of Columbia’s upcoming August ballot initiative, which seeks funding for additional police officers and firefighters. Carey stated that separating the votes prevents voter confusion because the city and county face entirely distinct law enforcement challenges.

If voters approve the three-eighths-cent tax in November, the revenue will fund a proposed 570-bed correctional campus designed to replace the current crumbling infrastructure. However, even with voter approval, construction will take four to five years, forcing the county to navigate an immediate and worsening fiscal storm.

A Shocking Fiscal Trajectory and the "Trailer Jail" Contingency

Boone County's financial burden from housing overflow inmates in neighboring jurisdictions has reached a breaking point. The county paid just over $900,000 for out-of-county housing in 2024. That figure exploded to more than $2.5 million in 2025. Based on first-quarter data, Carey now projects that out-of-county housing costs will skyrocket to between $4 million and $5 million by the end of the year.

The county currently pays $50 to $60 per day per inmate to outsource more than 200 detainees across 12 different Missouri counties, stretching as far south as Greene County.

The financial drain extends far beyond daily rental fees. The Sheriff's Office faces escalating staff overtime costs, vehicle wear-and-tear, and fuel expenses because deputies must constantly transport inmates across the state for court appearances and releases.

If voters reject the ballot measure, or as a temporary bridge during the multi-year construction phase, the county is exploring extreme stopgap measures. Carey and County Presiding Commissioner Kip Kendrick recently traveled to Norwood, Missouri, to inspect specialized modular units known as "trailer jails." These structures sit on parking lots and hook up to local utilities. Greene County previously utilized this identical system while constructing its own jail expansion years ago.

"This would be something similar to when the Columbia Public School District brings brown trailers onto a parking lot because they have too many students for the school," Carey explained. "We have to explore all options to help relieve some of this financial impact."

35 Years of Overcrowding and the Booking Surge

Public records show that jail overcrowding is a multi-decade crisis rather than a modern phenomenon. Boone County opened its current facility in January 1991 and breached its maximum capacity within four months. The county commissioned comprehensive overcrowding studies in 1997 and 2017—both led by former Judge Gary Oxenhandler—which concluded that an expanded brick-and-mortar facility was inevitable.

For years, county administrators determined that renting beds in neighboring counties cost less than funding a massive capital construction project. The arrival of COVID-19 in 2020 disrupted this calculation when social distancing requirements capped the jail’s internal capacity at roughly 100 detainees, forcing initial outsourcing spikes.

While city officials have publicly claimed that overall crime rates are falling, county booking data paints a drastically different picture. Hard data reveals an aggressive climb in local arrests. Bookings originating from the Columbia Police Department jumped from 1,864 in 2024 to 2,464 in 2025—a 32% single-year increase. Projections indicate that 2026 bookings will match or exceed that record pace.

Due Process Fractures and the State Mental Health Crisis

The logistical strain of housing prisoners hours away has severely degraded the functionality of the 13th Judicial Circuit Court system. Public defenders and private defense attorneys must travel hundreds of miles to consult with outsourced clients. These travel delays create a cog in the wheel of the criminal justice system, slowing case adjudications, lengthening pre-trial stays, and keeping defendants trapped in jail beds for prolonged periods.

Currently, an estimated 70% to 80% of all Boone County detainees reside in pre-trial detention, meaning they remain unconvicted and are simply awaiting trial or are unable to afford cash bail.

A severe statewide mental health crisis further compounds the space deficit. The Boone County Jail currently holds 25 detainees who are legally incompetent to stand trial and are waiting for the Missouri Department of Mental Health to provide state hospital beds. Because these individuals suffer from severe psychiatric crises and cannot enter the general inmate population, staff must house each of them alone in a traditional two-person cell. This bottleneck effectively robs the facility of 25 vital beds.

"Jails and prisons have become the new mental health facilities," Carey said, tracing the issue back to the federal and state deinstitutionalization movements of the 1960s and 1970s. "We have to address this problem at a local level because I am not confident that the state and federal governments are going to do anything to address this for us."

Correcting the Record: Prop L History and Future Phases

As the November vote approaches, county officials are actively correcting public misconceptions regarding past law enforcement taxes. Social media forums frequently miscite a "1999 Proposition L." County auditor archives confirm that voters actually approved the permanent one-eighth-cent Proposition L sales tax in August 2002, with implementation on Jan. 1, 2003.

Historical audits prove the county fulfilled every 2002 ballot promise, using the funds to hire 11 new sheriff’s deputies by late 2003 and purchasing now-standard fleet technologies like in-car video cameras and mobile data terminals.

The proposed three-eighths-cent tax will not feature a sunset clause. Officials designed the 570-bed facility to accommodate three future expansion phases over the next 15 to 40 years without disrupting active operations. Maintaining the tax ensures the county can fund these future phases internally without repeatedly returning to voters for money.

Carey also pushed back against critics who question the county's spending on recent capital projects, such as a local law enforcement training facility and a childcare center. State archives confirm that Gov. Mike Parson’s administration directly allocated $4 million in dedicated state funding for the regional training center in 2022 and $2.5 million for the childcare facility in 2023. Boone County could not legally redirect those earmarked state grants toward jail operations.

To appease criminal justice reform advocates, the proposed facility will feature a separate, dedicated Reentry Diversion Center. The center will actively divert non-violent individuals suffering from substance abuse and mental health crises out of the jail environment and into immediate treatment programs, aiming to permanently break the local cycle of recidivism.

Fudan University Graduates Under Shanghai lockdown: Where Do they Go after graduation?

This generation of young people has lived through a relatively stable era, so it is generally believed that certainty is the norm. However, if we look back a few decades, we can see that every generation has faced a great deal of uncertainty. Rather than focusing on adjustments, Professor Wang Yi of Fudan University suggests that we should reflect on what we still possess or clarify what we value most—such as our health and future prospects—while acknowledging what we have lost, ultimately holding onto hope.

2022-6-30 14:41 Shanghai

“Can you still graduate?” “Can you still graduate?” After carefully reviewing Liu Yi's thesis, her advisor posed this sharp question. Anxiety, unease, and avoidance have become part of Liu Yi's daily life as a graduate student.

At 8 a.m., awakened by a notice for antigen testing, Liu Yi began her day under quasi-closed grid management. Rubbing her sore eyes, she skillfully completed the antigen test, constantly anxious about the upcoming call from her advisor. “My thesis advisor is quite strict with me. Every time there are revision suggestions, they will directly call me via WeChat and give me a long list of revision requirements,” Liu Yi told the

reporter.

So far, she has only completed the video data coding for 30 thesis entries, which is still far short of the target of 240. However, the deadline for submitting the final thesis draft, May 26, is fast approaching. At present, the thesis is Liu Yi's biggest source of anxiety. The exceptionally strict thesis requirements have increased the difficulty of graduation, and the quasi-lockdown management has significantly reduced Liu Yi's efficiency. Even without thinking about it, the anxiety of not being able to complete the thesis and graduate on time constantly lingers in her mind.

The thesis is not the only source of anxiety for this year's graduates. For Xu Zhe, a mathematics major graduating this year, the pandemic in Shanghai has severely hindered the preparation of his study abroad materials, as all embassies are closed, and many documents cannot be processed. “I plan to study abroad for a master's degree, but our semester starts on July 5, leaving very little time to handle the visa and other documents.” Graduating in late May and already in the US by July, the rushed timeline left Xu Zhe no opportunity to fully savor the beauty of graduation season.

“I'm still waiting for updates on pandemic-related policies. If the current situation persists, I might have to return to my hometown to process the documents,” Xu Zhe sighed helplessly. Meanwhile, Hu Ming, a pharmacy student from the previous graduating class who had postponed graduation, faced similar challenges. Although he completed his thesis last year and received a satisfactory overseas offer this year, he was unable to prepare for language proficiency tests due to the pandemic. Due to the pandemic, IELTS exams were repeatedly canceled, resulting in his final language score being 0.5 points short. The school offered him a language course, meaning Hu Ming would need to spend additional time studying the language after arriving overseas. The extra time, effort, and expenses were an added burden for Hu Ming, who was already financially strained.

Beyond thesis anxiety and the high costs of studying abroad, many recent graduates are leaving school and entering the workforce. Xu Yuan, a Russian language major from the Class of 2018, secured a promising offer during last year's autumn recruitment season and will soon relocate to Shenzhen to work as a finance specialist at Huawei.

However, not everyone is so fortunate. Chu Weiran, another recent graduate from a less common language program, is currently facing challenges in her job search. Chu Weiran's language major does not have a broad market, and there are few job openings in her field. Although she started her own business during university and has her own social media account with over 10,000 followers, she does not have a significant advantage when job hunting. Graduating from a top university and having extensive entrepreneurial experience has not brought her the spotlight she hoped for. Facing repeated rejections and fluctuations in salary offers, Chu Weiran has been on the brink of collapse.

Amid the pandemic, many companies are struggling financially, and recent graduates navigating the job market are under immense pressure. While employment is achievable, when asked whether the positions align with their interests or offer satisfactory compensation, many students remain silent. After leaving Fudan University, they began exploring new paths.

The Absent Graduation Ceremony and the Breeze of Fudan Grass Time rewinds to the day of the quasi-lockdown, at the start of the semester, when students were filled with anticipation for graduation season. However, an unexpected notice turned everything upside down. On the morning of March 13 at 8 a.m., Fudan University issued Notice No. 1 of 2022 on the Implementation of Quasi-Closed Campus Management, announcing that quasi-closed campus management would be implemented starting at 8 p.m. on March 13, with faculty and students remaining on

campus.

Faculty and students were required to undergo nucleic acid testing (those off-campus were to test nearby and report results), with specific arrangements to be notified separately. The campus will be managed under a semi-closed system, with separate entrance areas. Faculty and students are not allowed to move between areas, and shuttle buses between campuses will be suspended. The semi-closed management period is temporarily set for two weeks, with the end date to be announced later.

Graduation parties, graduation ceremonies, graduation photos... all the beautiful memories associated with graduation have already faded away before they even began. Guangcao, NEO Bar, Xiaoma BBQ—these memories unique to Fudan students have not yet had the chance to be revisited. The semi-closed management system brings confusion and helplessness about the future.

At the beginning of the lockdown, Xu Zhe, a graduating student from the Mathematics Department, still imagined the graduation ceremony after the semi-closed management ended. As a daily source of hope, Xu Zhe used this aspiration to motivate himself to study hard and conduct research, hoping that each new day would be better. However, by late March, the COVID-19 outbreak in Shanghai escalated, and the date for lifting the semi-closed management was repeatedly postponed, eventually becoming indefinite. Xu Zhe began to doubt himself.

“I consider myself a relatively calm person who doesn't easily lose control of my emotions, but during that period, I felt like I didn't want to do anything and had no motivation.” In early April, when the pandemic was at its worst, Xu Zhe missed many discussions about his thesis. He just wanted to lie in bed and rest, not wanting to do anything.

If it weren't for the pandemic, Xu Zhe would have invited his friends to dine together, go out, and take graduation photos. “I always thought that the graduation season was a wonderful thing during the four years of university, where we could spend our final days together and create beautiful shared memories.” However, this year, these graduation wishes are difficult to fulfill. For Xu Zhe, the pandemic has been severe for the past 20 years, so an online graduation season was expected; but the sudden outbreak of the pandemic in Shanghai shattered all his beautiful aspirations for graduation. At this pivotal moment of graduation, a perfect conclusion was ultimately missing.

Liu Yi from the Psychology Department also experienced a food poisoning incident. In mid-April, the meals in Liu Yi's dormitory building had some issues, causing many students to develop acute gastroenteritis with vomiting and diarrhea. Additionally, since the building had previously had close contacts, management was particularly strict, further dampening Liu Yi's mood. “Every day was a routine life, unsure of what the future held, as if time had come to a standstill.” A month ago, Liu Yi shared her graduation checklist on social media: she wanted to dye her hair pink and go out with friends... Now, all of that seems like a distant dream.

Chronic anxiety under the pandemic The pandemic has brought numerous challenges to graduates, and the strict management measures have exacerbated many underlying psychological issues. Xu Yuan, a senior from the Class of 2018, believes that graduation anxiety has always existed. Although the proportion of graduation anxiety at top universities may be lower than at ordinary schools, in recent years, due to the pandemic and economic cycles, employment conditions have been worse than in the past. Therefore, compared to previous graduating classes before the pandemic, this year's class has a higher proportion of anxiety.

In fact, the “stay-at-home” management regulations have deprived students of the

opportunity to vent their frustrations, and issues that could have been resolved are now magnified several times over, causing many students to lose their mental balance. Zhang Meng, a graduate student in physics and electronics from the Class of 2019, strongly agrees. For a long time, students in the northern campus were completely unable to go out, and during that period, she felt extremely distressed, even to the point of not seeing the sun every day. Zhang Meng spent her days confined to her dormitory, facing numerous restrictions and unable to eat good food, which left her in a very bad mood.

“At first, I tried really hard to suppress those feelings, telling myself I had to stay optimistic and keep a positive mindset. But I realized that didn’t really work. It’s better to just let yourself cry when you feel like it, allowing your emotions to release, turning that psychological burden into a physical release.” When her mood was at its lowest, Zhang Meng would allow herself to cry, giving some relief to the immense pressure she felt.

For students at top universities, anxiety is always present, and the pressure from all sides leaves them feeling suffocated. Zhang Meng’s emotional anxiety primarily stems from the people around her; she is often influenced by the emotions of her classmates. Additionally, some of Zhang Meng’s classmates are renting apartments and interning off-campus, while others have already published several papers in top-tier journals. Zhang Meng can’t help but compare herself to others.

However, each student has their own way of relaxing. Xu Zhe chooses to play online board games with his close friends, such as murder mystery games, werewolf games, and “you draw, I guess.” Hearing the familiar voices of his friends, exchanging updates, and encouraging each other makes him feel relaxed.

Pan Yijun, a graduate student in Medical Imaging and Nuclear Medicine from the Class

of 2019, chooses to volunteer, conducting nucleic acid tests for her classmates and distributing boxed meals. Often, a fulfilling half-day passes by. Pan Yijun also enjoys following Liu Genghong's workouts, and after finishing a song like “Bencao Gangmu,” she feels much more relaxed.

Some students watch movies or TV shows and discuss them with their roommates. Liu Yi, a psychology major from the Class of 2018, loves watching “The Legend of Zhenhuan,” and her favorite characters are Shen Meizhuang and Ye Lanyi. “They all dislike the emperor, and this spirit of resistance really impresses me.” Perhaps, confined to the small space of the dormitory, she also wants to restore a sense of normalcy through her efforts. Under the grid management system, each student is striving to adjust their mental state, trying to make the mundane aspects of life more fulfilling.

“I will seek for my goals” In fact, aside from a few students who only begin to think about their future plans near graduation, most students have already determined their future direction before graduation season arrives. Those planning to study abroad have prepared their materials and applications, those pursuing graduate school have been working on research projects with their advisors for some time, and those deciding to enter the workforce immediately after graduation have already set their career goals by their sophomore or junior year and begun internships early on.

However, not every student has a clear self-awareness. In the mathematics department, many of Xu Zhe's classmates are relatively reserved. Quiet and focused on research— these are the stereotypes often associated with mathematics students. For students in these foundational disciplines, anxiety does not stem solely from the future but more from uncertainty about their future path.

Failure in graduate school entrance exams, forced deferment of graduation, and the

disconnect between their major and career are the severe issues they face. At its core, they have not actively explored their future; instead, they are often driven by the curriculum and school policies. Even when they reach a certain point, they still do not know what will happen next.

“What our classmates lack is often the mindset of actively exploring. They are more focused on completing the requirements of the curriculum. They haven't considered what they truly want to do, whether they are suited for a career change, pursuing interdisciplinary studies, entering finance, teaching, or civil service. They haven't thought about these things. They are being pushed forward unwillingly, and few have taken the initiative to think for themselves,” Xu Zhe described the challenges his classmates are facing.

Clear self-awareness is also a core issue. As graduation approaches, many students begin to contemplate their future paths. Some choose to pursue graduate or doctoral studies to avoid entering the workforce prematurely. However, after enrolling in graduate school, their concerns often narrow to whether they can complete their doctoral studies and, after doing so, whether their peers have already achieved career success and surpassed them.

These questions reflect a lack of proactive exploration. When faced with future choices, Xu Zhe offered his own answer. A near-perfect GPA and outstanding academic ability are not Xu Zhe's only labels. His sunny disposition, openness, extensive volunteer activities, and multiple quantitative internship experiences are just some of his many experiences. Starting from his sophomore and junior years, Xu Zhe actively sought out outstanding students from other majors to observe how they approached their studies, learning from their qualities and ways of thinking.

A strong sense of self-respect prevents him from falling behind. Xu Zhe has consistently

strived to be among the top performers and has made his university life exceptionally fulfilling. However, he believes the most important aspect is to reflect on oneself, clarify one's true interests, and execute plans with unwavering determination.

This July, Xu Zhe will be on the campus of MIT. As graduation approaches, whether actively or passively, everyone must reflect on where they are heading in the future. Graduation is merely a milestone in the long journey of life, and each person’s life is inseparable from the pursuit of self-discovery and proactive exploration of the external world. After leaving Fudan, each student enters a new environment and continues to contemplate the central themes of their own lives.

Dialogue with the School: Career Development Plan In terms of career management, Wang Yi, the head of the Student Affairs Office at the School of Journalism, introduced the school's employment support policies. The school offers one-on-one career counseling to help students revise their resumes and identify their strengths; there are also career development lectures for the majority of students, providing them with employment information summaries and job-seeking strategy planning.

Additionally, the school's New Voice Salon provides a platform for students to connect with the outside world. The New Voice Salon periodically invites senior students and alumni from various industries to share their experiences, covering diverse fields with rich content. There is also the Media Literacy Up Station, which helps students enhance their overall job-seeking abilities, including oral communication skills and writing abilities, with the aim of improving their media literacy.

Professor Wang Yi believes that the uncertainty brought by the pandemic is not the only factor affecting the employment of recent graduates. "We are facing a time of significant change, with both international and domestic circumstances constantly evolving. The

entire global community is currently experiencing this uncertainty. " In Professor Wang Yi's view, this is the second time we have faced the uncertainty brought by the pandemic. The graduates of 2020 have already experienced it once. Every era and every generation faces its own uncertainties.

This generation of young people has lived in a relatively stable era, so they generally perceive certainty as the norm. However, if we look back a few decades, we see that many people in every generation have faced numerous uncertainties. Rather than focusing on adjustments, Professor Wang Yi believes we should reflect on what we still have while acknowledging what we have lost. Or, to clarify what we value most—such as our health and the hope for a better future—we should remain optimistic. In summary, while we have lost certain things, this is also a normal part of life. Everyone must learn to confront and accept the uncertainty of their generation.

The Challenge of Employment for College Students Currently, college students still face numerous challenges in their job searches. From the students' perspective, many still hold the belief that employment should be a lifelong commitment. Many college graduates would rather stay at home than seek employment, as they aspire to find a career they can dedicate their lives to and a job that brings them great satisfaction. The notion of only seeking employment within the public sector is deeply rooted in traditional ideas about “iron rice bowls.”

College students or their parents urgently hope to enter the public sector, as the idea of a dignified, stable, and traditional career is deeply ingrained. Especially with the development of the pandemic in 2020, people have become even more eager to enter the public sector to avoid the risk of unemployment.

Lastly, there is a lack of innovative thinking, with a focus solely on job selection rather than entrepreneurship. The employment mindset among college students is deeply

ingrained, with the traditional notion that attending university is solely for the purpose of securing a good job. However, given the current high employment pressure, while the government encourages young people to start their own businesses, the vast majority of college students lack the innovative and adventurous spirit required for entrepreneurship, as well as the potential to pursue it.

Additionally, college students lack the cultivation of core employment skills. Employment skills refer to an individual's ability to perform one or multiple jobs. For college students, employment skills refer to their ability to find and excel in a job suitable for them, narrowly defined as the ability to apply for and secure employment. From enrollment to graduation, both individuals and schools focus on mastering professional courses, neglecting the cultivation of core employment skills.

This results in many college students facing repeated setbacks when job hunting or encountering difficulties during internships. The cultivation of employment skills is not something that can be achieved through a single employment education class or a brief internship period. It should be made a routine and important task.

From a macro perspective, the overall reduction in available job positions is also a factor affecting the employment prospects of recent graduates. Due to the pandemic, the number of available job positions has significantly decreased. Private enterprises and small and medium-sized businesses have been particularly hard-hit by the pandemic, and this sector—which is a key employer of university graduates—relies heavily on market conditions and has weak risk-bearing capacity.

Operational difficulties have led to layoffs and hiring freezes. The real economy, including machinery manufacturing, energy, mining, import-export trade, and logistics companies, has been hit hardest by the decline in hiring demand, with wages and benefits generally decreasing and talent turnover increasing. The COVID-19 pandemic

has led to a high proportion of labor mobility, necessitating employment guidance that aligns with the current economic landscape, redefines priorities, and focuses on specific career goals.

The post-pandemic reality has further exacerbated the existing employment pressures faced by college graduates. For example, some students lack clear self-awareness and long-term planning, blindly following trends in graduate school entrance exams or civil service exams, and overly relying on societal expectations for career choices. Discrepancies between self-perception and societal expectations make them more prone to irrational behaviors such as “overestimating one's abilities” or “short-sightedness” in the increasingly competitive graduate job market. Conversely, there is another extreme: excessive pessimism and anxiety about employment, leading graduates to rush to seize any “opportunity” and blindly choose jobs that are below their qualifications.

Currently, university employment information platforms have not been effectively developed, utilized, or promoted. Employment services integrated with information technology can achieve more precise and efficient information matching, but this requires staff involved in employment guidance to have the ability to manage multiple platforms. In fact, most university employment guidance centers do not have dedicated online operations personnel, and frontline counselors lack relevant training, resulting in low utilization rates of online platforms such as websites, Weibo, live streams, and short videos.

From the perspective of existing policies, employment support efforts lack “precision.” Achieving “precise employment” essentially means achieving employment where everyone can contribute to their full potential. “Precision” implies that the employment needs of every student can be met. Currently, students' employment needs are diverse, and career expectations vary depending on the individual, the time, and the

circumstances. These needs intertwine material and spiritual needs, short-term and long-term needs, individual and social needs, and so on. Additionally, as the employment process progresses, employment concepts often change, making it challenging to precisely meet students' needs. Additionally, the current domestic and international economic landscape has been significantly impacted by the pandemic, and China is continuously advancing industrial structure adjustments and employment policy reforms. These issues have become even more pronounced in the post-pandemic era, posing significant challenges to “precise employment.”

Looking ahead, Liu Yi plans to conduct in-depth research in the field of science and technology, Xu Zhe will pursue further studies in financial engineering in the United States, and Xu Yuan will head to Shenzhen to officially kickstart his career. During graduation season, everyone is scattered across the globe, and the sudden pandemic even deprived them of the chance to bid farewell. Facing uncertainty, every Fudan graduate must now begin writing their own new story.
